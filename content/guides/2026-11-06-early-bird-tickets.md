Early bird tickets are cheaper admission tickets for people who buy first, available only in a limited number or until a fixed date. They exist to get sales moving on day one: you see early whether the event lands, you build word of mouth, and you give hesitant buyers a reason to act now. They work best as a separate ticket type with a fixed allocation, for example "Early bird – 80 tickets", priced noticeably below standard and followed by a standard tier and a door price. This guide covers tier structures, sizing, timing, honest scarcity and the EU pricing rules that apply (as of November 2026).

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - An early bird ticket gives the same admission as a standard ticket. Only the price and the time of purchase differ.
> - Common starting points are an allocation of 10–25 % of capacity and a discount of 15–25 % on the standard price. These are rules of thumb, not research findings.
> - Check the early bird price against your costs: after VAT and fees it must still contribute to your fixed costs.
> - Scarcity must be true. False "only today" claims are blacklisted under the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive.
> - Name two real prices ("€12 early bird, then €15") instead of a struck-through "was" price.

## What does "early bird" mean for tickets?

**Early bird** is an advance-purchase price: whoever commits first pays less. An **early bird ticket** is offered at the start of sales and ends either when a fixed number is sold or on a fixed date. After that, the standard price applies.

Unlike a VIP or concession ticket, it gives exactly the same entry as a standard ticket. That is what makes it fair: nobody gets less, and early buyers are rewarded for their trust.

### Early bird vs general admission

"General admission" (GA) describes the type of access, typically unreserved standing or seating. "Early bird" describes the price point. An early bird ticket is usually an early, cheaper GA ticket: same door, same area, lower price, smaller allocation.

### Why early bird pricing works

- **Proof of demand.** The first days show whether interest is strong enough. If sales are slow, you still have time to adjust marketing or costs.
- **Momentum.** Every early buyer tells friends. Visible early sales signal that the event is really happening.
- **Less last-minute risk.** The more you sell in advance, the less your evening depends on weather or a rival event.
- **Cash flow, with a caveat.** Many platforms, Eventoren included, pay out only after the event. Do not plan venue deposits around ticket money.

## Which early bird tier structures can you use?

Early bird is usually the first step of a price ladder. These structures are common:

| Model                | Tiers                                      | Switch trigger      | Good fit                                         |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Classic three-step   | Early bird → standard → door               | allocation sold out | concerts, parties, club nights, community events |
| Four-step            | Early bird → standard → late → door        | allocation or date  | festivals, large parties with long sales periods |
| Early bird waves     | Early bird 1 → early bird 2 → standard     | allocation sold out | events with a loyal returning crowd              |
| Deadline             | Early registration until date X → standard | fixed date          | conferences, workshops, balls, galas             |
| Exclusive early bird | Presale with code → public sale            | end of presale      | members, fan clubs, newsletter subscribers       |

Allocation-based tiers are easiest for buyers to understand: "The first 80 tickets cost €12." Date-based tiers are easier to plan around, but you must switch on time. For member or fan presales, see our guide to [presale codes](/en/guides/presale-codes).

## How many early bird tickets, and how much cheaper?

The two main levers are allocation size and the gap to the standard price. There are no fixed rules; these are starting points from practice that you should test with your own audience.

| Lever                    | Typical starting point | Too small                        | Too large                                            |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Allocation               | 10–25 % of capacity    | gone in minutes, feels like bait | standard sales start too late, margin leaks          |
| Discount on standard     | 15–25 %                | no reason to buy early           | standard buyers feel penalised, contribution shrinks |
| Duration (if date-based) | 1–4 weeks after launch | hardly anyone notices            | no urgency, decisions get postponed                  |

### Discount examples

Worked example: three discount levels on four standard prices, before rounding.

| Standard price | Early bird at −15 % | at −20 % | at −25 % |
| -------------- | ------------------- | -------- | -------- |
| €12            | €10.20              | €9.60    | €9.00    |
| €15            | €12.75              | €12.00   | €11.25   |
| €20            | €17.00              | €16.00   | €15.00   |
| €30            | €25.50              | €24.00   | €22.50   |

Round to clean amounts. "€12" sticks; "€12.75" does not. And communicate the euro amount, not the percentage (see the legal section below).

## Does the early bird price still cover your costs?

An early bird price can be low, but never below your variable costs. Worked example: a 400-capacity concert in Lyon, admission taxed at the French 5.5 % rate for shows, organiser absorbs a 3.9 % + €0.49 ticketing fee (as of November 2026).

| Tier       | Allocation | Price | Net of VAT | Fee   | Left per ticket | Total         |
| ---------- | ---------- | ----- | ---------- | ----- | --------------- | ------------- |
| Early bird | 80         | €12   | €11.37     | €0.96 | €10.41          | €832.80       |
| Standard   | 240        | €15   | €14.22     | €1.08 | €13.14          | €3,153.60     |
| Door       | 80         | €18   | €17.06     | €1.19 | €15.87          | €1,269.60     |
| **Total**  | **400**    |       |            |       |                 | **€5,256.00** |

A flat €15 for all 400 tickets would leave €5,256.00, exactly the same. The early bird costs about €218 compared with standard, and the door tier wins it back. But if only 30 door tickets sell, total income drops to €4,462.50. So check that your standard tier alone reaches break-even, and treat the door as upside. The full method is in [how to price event tickets](/en/guides/how-to-price-event-tickets).

## When should you launch and end the early bird?

Launch it when attention peaks: the moment you announce the event. A typical sequence:

1. **Teaser (1–2 weeks before):** hint at date and venue, announce when sales open ("Early bird from Friday, 6 pm").
2. **Early bird launch:** publish the line-up or programme, share the ticket link everywhere at once: website, newsletter, Instagram, WhatsApp or Telegram channels.
3. **During the early bird:** post honest progress updates, for example when about half are gone.
4. **End of early bird:** announce that standard prices now apply. No silent switch.
5. **Second push (optional):** new information such as a support act gives standard sales a fresh reason to buy.

How long total sales should run depends on the format. Club nights often sell over two to four weeks, concerts and balls over several months, and festivals over half a year or more.

### What to say: weak vs strong wording

| Weak                              | Better                                                |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| "Grab your tickets now!"          | "The first 80 tickets cost €12, then €15."            |
| "Early bird for a limited time"   | "Early bird until Sunday 23:59 or until 80 are sold." |
| "Almost sold out!" (at 30 % sold) | "20 early bird tickets left." (when true)             |
| "Early bird −20 %"                | "Early bird €12, standard €15, door €18."             |

Announce every tier from the start. Buyers can then see why buying early pays off, and nobody feels ambushed when the price rises. That is exactly what the UK Competition and Markets Authority secured from Ticketmaster after the 2024 Oasis sale: fans must be told at least 24 hours in advance if tiered pricing is used, and see the price range when joining a queue. ## Which EU rules apply to early bird pricing?

This guide is not legal or tax advice. As of November 2026, three pieces of EU law matter most for early bird campaigns:

| Rule                                                                                   | What it says                                                                                                                    | What it means for early birds                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| UCPD Annex I, No. 7 (Directive 2005/29/EC)                                             | Falsely stating that a product is available, or available on particular terms, only for a very limited time is always unfair    | "Only today" must be true. If you announce an end date, keep it                                                                                                                   |
| UCPD Art. 6(1)(d)                                                                      | A practice is misleading if it deceives about the price or the existence of a specific price advantage                          | A struck-through "was €25" that never existed is misleading, for goods and services alike                                                                                         |
| UCPD Art. 7(4)(c)                                                                      | In an invitation to purchase, the price inclusive of taxes is material information                                              | Show each tier's total price including mandatory fees, not just the base price                                                                                                    |
| Price Indication Directive 98/6/EC, Art. 6a (added by the Omnibus Directive 2019/2161) | Any announced price reduction must state the prior price, meaning the lowest price in at least the 30 days before the reduction | Written for products covered by that directive, i.e. goods. Germany's transposition (§ 11 PAngV) refers explicitly to goods, so a direct application to event tickets is doubtful |

The practical conclusion is the same everywhere: don't frame an early bird as a reduction from an invented price. Two named prices, "early bird €12, standard €15", are clear for buyers and avoid the 30-day question entirely. National rules may go further, for example Belgium's 2013 ticket resale law requires primary sellers to show the final price on the ticket.

### Using scarcity honestly

- **Quantities must be real.** "20 left" only when 20 are left.
- **Deadlines must hold.** Silently extending an early bird you announced "until Sunday" makes the announcement untrue in hindsight. End it and start a clearly named new tier instead, such as "Early bird 2".
- **Don't fake tiers.** A "premium" label needs a real extra benefit. The CMA criticised "platinum" tickets that offered nothing more than some standard tickets in the same area.

## Common early bird mistakes

- [ ] **No real end:** an early bird that runs until the week of the event is simply your price.
- [ ] **Allocation too large:** if half of all tickets are early bird, you give away margin.
- [ ] **Allocation too small:** ten tickets gone in three minutes frustrate more people than they please.
- [ ] **Gap too small:** 50 cents is no reason to buy early.
- [ ] **Silent switch:** without an announcement, the "price goes up now" effect is wasted.
- [ ] **Door cheaper than standard:** this punishes advance buyers and trains your audience to wait.
- [ ] **Allocations above capacity:** early bird, standard, guest list and door together must not exceed it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does early bird mean on tickets?

It means an advance-purchase price. An early bird ticket is a cheaper ticket available only at the start of sales, either for a fixed number of tickets or until a set date. It gives the same admission as a standard ticket.

### How much cheaper should early bird tickets be?

A common starting range is 15–25 % below the standard price, rounded to a clean amount. With a €15 standard price, €12 is a typical example. Check beforehand that the early bird price still contributes to your fixed costs after VAT and fees.

### How many early bird tickets should I offer?

A reasonable starting point is 10–25 % of capacity. If they sell out in minutes, the allocation was too small or the price too low. If they are still available after weeks, the incentive was too weak or the allocation too big.

### Can I advertise early bird tickets with a struck-through price?

It is risky and unnecessary. A reference price that was never charged is misleading under the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, for services as well as goods. Naming the early bird and standard price as two separate prices is clearer and avoids the problem.

### Can I extend an early bird deadline?

Avoid it if you announced a fixed end, because the announcement becomes untrue in hindsight and false time limits are blacklisted under EU law. End the tier as promised and, if needed, open a clearly named new one such as "Early bird 2" with its own allocation.
