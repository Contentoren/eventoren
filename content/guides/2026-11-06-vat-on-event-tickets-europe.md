Event tickets in Europe carry the VAT rate of the country where the event takes place, which for concerts and theatre is usually a reduced rate: 7 % in Germany, 5.5 % in France, 9 % in the Netherlands, 13 % in Austria. Sport, parties and livestreams are often taxed differently. Rates and rules below are as of November 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Physical events: admission is taxed where the event takes place (VAT Directive Art. 53 for B2B, Art. 54 for B2C).
> - Virtual events since 2025: B2C tickets are taxed where the consumer lives; B2B virtual admission follows the customer's location.
> - Reduced rates for concerts and theatre in most EU countries; sport, DJ parties and dinner packages often differ. Denmark taxes concerts and theatre at 25 %, and Ireland exempts many live performances.
> - Events in another EU country mean charging that country's VAT. B2C admission can be declared via the OSS; check local registration duties.
> - Booking fees you charge as organiser usually follow the ticket's rate; a platform's own service fee is a separate supply.

This guide is not legal or tax advice.

## Where is VAT due on an event ticket?

**Place of supply** is the country whose VAT applies. For admission tickets, the EU VAT Directive (2006/112/EC, consolidated 14 April 2025) overrides the usual "where the customer is" logic.

| Ticket                    | Buyer          | Place of supply                                                                   | Rule                                                                     |
| ------------------------- | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Physical event            | business (B2B) | where the event actually takes place                                              | Art. 53                                                                  |
| Physical event            | consumer (B2C) | where the event actually takes place                                              | Art. 54(1)                                                               |
| Streamed or virtual event | business (B2B) | where the business customer is established (general rule)                         | Art. 44, since Art. 53 no longer applies to virtual attendance from 2025 |
| Streamed or virtual event | consumer (B2C) | where the consumer is established, has their permanent address or usually resides | Art. 54(1), as amended from 1 January 2025                               |

**Admission** covers entry to cultural, artistic, sporting, scientific, educational, entertainment or similar events, from concerts and matches to conferences and fairs, plus ancillary services such as cloakrooms.

So a Dutch promoter selling tickets for a show in Antwerp charges Belgian VAT, while a German club streaming to fans in France and Spain charges each buyer's local rate.

## Do foreign organisers need to register for VAT in the event country?

If you're a VAT-registered business and sell tickets for an event in another EU country, you owe that country's VAT. How you pay it depends on who buys.

### Sales to consumers: the One-Stop Shop

Since 1 July 2021, the **Union OSS scheme** lets an EU business declare B2C services supplied in other Member States through one quarterly return at home. The Commission's OSS explanatory notes list "admission to cultural, artistic, sporting, scientific, educational, entertainment or similar events" among the B2C services that can be declared under the scheme. If you use the OSS, you must declare all eligible supplies through it.

Three caveats:

- **No OSS threshold for admission.** The EUR 10,000 threshold only covers electronic services and distance sales of goods. Admission abroad is taxable from the first euro.
- **Local VAT on your costs** (venue hire, catering, staging) can't be deducted via the OSS return. You reclaim it through the EU refund procedure or a local VAT return.
- **Local practice and other duties.** Some countries may still expect local registration, for example if you also sell to businesses, run a bar or have a fixed establishment there. Check before you go on sale.

### Sales to businesses

For B2B admission (Art. 53), VAT is also due in the event country. Whether the buyer accounts for it (reverse charge) or you must register locally depends on that country's rules, so check national guidance.

### Organisers outside the EU

Non-EU organisers, including UK organisers selling tickets for EU events, can use the **non-Union OSS scheme** for B2C services, according to the same notes. EU organisers running events in the UK or Switzerland must follow UK or Swiss rules.

## How is VAT applied to booking and service fees?

As a rule of thumb:

| Fee                                                                  | Typical VAT treatment                                                                                | Basis                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Booking fee that the organiser charges as part of the ticket price   | ancillary to admission, so same place and rate as the ticket                                         | in Germany: BFH V R 16/09                             |
| Platform service fee charged to the buyer in the platform's own name | separate supply by the platform, usually at the standard rate                                        | derived from German case law; check national guidance |
| Platform fee charged to the organiser (B2B)                          | service to the organiser, usually standard rate; cross-border B2B fees are generally reverse-charged | in Germany: BFH V B 98/25                             |
| Cancellation or refund fee                                           | depends on whether it is consideration for a service; check national guidance                        |                                                       |

## Worked examples

All examples use a gross ticket price that includes VAT. VAT = gross price × rate ÷ (100 + rate). Example figures only.

### One €40 concert ticket across Europe

| Event country                                       | Rate (concert) | Net price | VAT   |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | --------- | ----- |
| France                                              | 5.5 %          | €37.91    | €2.09 |
| Belgium                                             | 6 %            | €37.74    | €2.26 |
| Germany                                             | 7 %            | €37.38    | €2.62 |
| Poland                                              | 8 %            | €37.04    | €2.96 |
| Netherlands                                         | 9 %            | €36.70    | €3.30 |
| Italy, Spain                                        | 10 %           | €36.36    | €3.64 |
| Austria                                             | 13 %           | €35.40    | €4.60 |
| Ireland (food or drink available; otherwise exempt) | 13.5 %         | €35.24    | €4.76 |
| Denmark (unless an approved charity event)          | 25 %           | €32.00    | €8.00 |

On 500 tickets, the gap between France and Denmark is €2,955 of VAT on the same gross revenue. Work backwards from the gross price, using the event country's rate.

### German organiser, concert in Vienna

A VAT-registered German promoter sells 300 tickets at €30 for a concert in Vienna to consumers. The place of supply is Austria, so Austrian VAT at 13 % applies, not German 7 %. Per ticket: net €26.55, VAT €3.45, so €1,035 of Austrian VAT in total. The promoter declares it via the Union OSS from Germany or registers in Austria, and reclaims Austrian VAT on the venue hire separately.

### Livestream sold to several countries

A Belgian theatre company sells a €10 livestream ticket to consumers. Since 2025, each sale is taxed in the buyer's country, and whether a reduced rate covers streams varies (Ireland, for example, taxes streamed events at the standard rate). At 21 %, VAT is €1.74 per ticket; at 20 %, €1.67. The company can declare all foreign B2C sales through the OSS in Belgium.

## Checklist: VAT before you go on sale abroad

- [ ] Event type defined: concert, theatre, sport, party, conference, livestream
- [ ] Place of supply confirmed: event country (physical) or buyer's country (virtual B2C)
- [ ] Rate confirmed with the event country's tax authority or an adviser
- [ ] Exemptions checked if you are a public, cultural or non-profit body
- [ ] Decided between OSS and local registration; noted the quarterly OSS deadlines
- [ ] Plan for reclaiming foreign VAT on venue and supplier costs
- [ ] Booking fees and bundles (ticket plus dinner or drinks) priced and taxed separately where needed
- [ ] Ticket prices shown to consumers as totals including all fees
- [ ] Invoices for business buyers set up; e-invoicing duties checked for each country

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you charge VAT on event tickets?

Yes, if you're a VAT-registered business, unless an exemption applies, for example to some non-profit bodies or, in Ireland, to many live performances. Small businesses under their home small-business scheme may not charge VAT at home; whether that also covers an event abroad depends on the event country, so check national guidance.

### Which country's VAT applies to a ticket for an event abroad?

The country where the event physically takes place, for both consumers and businesses. Only streamed or virtual events differ: since 2025, B2C tickets are taxed in the buyer's country.

### Can I use the OSS for tickets to an event in another EU country?

According to the European Commission's OSS explanatory notes, B2C admission to events can be declared through the OSS by a business not established in the event country. Local VAT on costs can't be reclaimed through the OSS, and sales to businesses are not covered. Confirm the set-up with the event country's tax authority.

### What VAT applies to a livestream ticket?

Since 1 January 2025, a livestream ticket sold to a consumer is taxed where the consumer lives, at that country's rate. Whether the reduced rate for live performances also covers streams depends on the country, so check national guidance.

### Is VAT on concert tickets the same as on sports tickets?

Not always. Many countries tax both at the same reduced rate (Austria, the Netherlands, Belgium, France, Poland). Germany applies 19 % to commercial sports events but 7 % to concerts, Spain applies 10 % only to amateur sport, and Denmark taxes concerts at 25 % unless a charity event exemption has been approved. In Ireland, both are often exempt.
