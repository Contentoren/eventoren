The best Eventbrite alternative in Europe depends on where you pay out and what you sell. For low fees on cheap tickets, Eventfrog Free and Weezevent cost less than Eventbrite in most countries. For Dutch and Belgian audiences, Weeztix is strong. For seat maps and a box office in Germany, EVENTIM.Light and pretix fit, and for an all-inclusive euro checkout with browser-based scanning, Eventoren is one option. Eventbrite has belonged to Bending Spoons since 10 March 2026, has closed its Pro plan to new subscribers and charges very different fees from one European country to the next. This guide shows the facts, the numbers and when staying put is the right call, as of November 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Bending Spoons closed its acquisition of Eventbrite on 10 March 2026 at $4.50 per share (about $500 M). Eventbrite is no longer listed on the NYSE.
> - Pro is closed to new subscribers. The new Eventbrite Premium costs €59/month in Germany, France, Ireland, Italy and Spain, rolled out gradually.
> - Eventbrite fees depend on your payout country: 2 % + €0.49 in the Netherlands, 5.5 % in Germany and 5.9 % + €0.79 in Ireland (Essentials, as of November 2026).
> - Eventbrite still fits if you need marketplace discovery, waitlists, multi-currency sales or reserved seating.
> - Switch between events, not mid-presale, and export your attendee data first.

## What changed at Eventbrite in 2026?

### Pro closed, Premium launched

Eventbrite has closed its **Pro** subscription to new subscribers. Its successor, **Eventbrite Premium**, costs €59 per month or €588 per year in Germany, France, Ireland, Italy and Spain (£59 in the UK). It includes up to 2,000 marketing emails per day, a $20 monthly ads credit and 50 % off instant-payout fees. Eventbrite says the rollout is gradual, starting with the US. We found no change to per-ticket fees for 2025/26.

For you, that means the per-ticket fee stays the same. But if you relied on Eventbrite's email marketing, budget for the subscription.

## How much does Eventbrite cost in each European country?

Eventbrite sets fees by **payout country**, not by where your buyers live. Most EU countries are on "legacy packages" called Essentials and Professional. As of November 2026:

| Payout country             | Essentials     | Professional  | Fee on €20 (Essentials, absorbed) |
| -------------------------- | -------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Netherlands (ticket > €10) | 2 % + €0.49    | 4 % + €0.99   | €0.89                             |
| Belgium                    | 3 % + €0.49    | 4 % + €0.99   | €1.09                             |
| France, Italy, Portugal    | 3.5 % + €0.49  | 5.5 % + €0.99 | €1.19                             |
| Germany, Austria           | 5.5 %          | 5.5 % + €0.99 | €1.10                             |
| Spain                      | 6 %            | 9 % + €0.19   | €1.20                             |
| Ireland                    | 5.9 % + €0.79  | –             | €1.97                             |
| Poland                     | 1 % + zł2.99   | –             | not in EUR                        |
| UK                         | 6.95 % + £0.59 | –             | not in EUR                        |

The €20 column is our own calculation: percentage × €20 + fixed fee. Eventbrite lists no separate payment processing fee for these packages, so we treat it as included. Whether the German 5.5 % includes VAT isn't stated and remains unverified. If 19 % VAT were added, it would be €1.31 instead of €1.10 (example calculation). Donation tickets cost 2 % in several countries, and free events are free.

By default, buyers pay the fee on top. You can tick "Absorb fees" to include it in your price.

## When is Eventbrite still the right choice?

Eventbrite has real strengths that a smaller European platform doesn't replace. Stay, or choose it on purpose, if one of these matters most:

- **Discovery.** Eventbrite says it reaches 89 M monthly users. For workshops, meet-ups and networking events in cities like Dublin, Amsterdam or Berlin, that can bring extra sales.
- **Low Dutch and Belgian rates.** With a Dutch payout, Essentials costs €0.89 on a €20 ticket, cheaper than almost every alternative.
- **Many currencies and countries.** Eventbrite pays out in AT, BE, CZ, DK, FI, FR, DE, GR, HU, IE, IT, NL, PL, PT, ES, SE, CH and NO, plus the UK and beyond.
- **Waitlists, reminders and reserved seating** are part of its feature set.

Common reasons organisers leave are cost (especially in Germany, Spain and Ireland), the lack of EUR door sales (Eventbrite's in-app door sales work only for AUD, CAD, GBP and USD events), uncertainty after the takeover, and wanting a platform closer to local rules and languages.

## How do you switch from Eventbrite without losing sales?

Switch between events, not halfway through a presale. Tickets already sold normally stay valid on Eventbrite, so you start the next event on the new platform.

- [ ] **Map live events:** let any event with an open presale finish on Eventbrite.
- [ ] **Export data:** download attendee lists and orders before you cancel a subscription.
- [ ] **Check consents:** only contacts who opted in may receive marketing emails. Existing customers fall under the narrow ePrivacy soft opt-in.
- [ ] **Cancel plans:** check whether you're on Pro or Premium and when it renews.
- [ ] **Decide the fee model:** absorb or pass on, and recalculate your ticket price.
- [ ] **Rebuild ticket types:** capacities, early bird tiers, group and member prices.
- [ ] **Match payment methods:** Bancontact for Belgium, iDEAL or Wero for the Netherlands, BLIK for Poland. See [ticket payment methods in Europe](/en/guides/ticket-payment-methods-europe).
- [ ] **Replace links:** website, Instagram bio, link pages, Google Business Profile and email signatures.
- [ ] **Test the door:** buy a test ticket and scan it with the new scanner. Brief your helpers.
- [ ] **Plan the payout:** add your IBAN and allow time for organiser verification.

Allow one to two weeks before your on-sale date, because many platforms, Eventoren included, check organisations before the first sale. For the full setup from scratch, see [how to sell tickets online in Europe](/en/guides/sell-tickets-online).

## How to do this with Eventoren

Eventoren is an Eventbrite alternative for organisers who sell in euros. It charges 3.9 % of the gross ticket price plus €0.49 per paid ticket, including VAT and payment processing, with no base fee, no minimum revenue and no minimum term. Free tickets cost nothing, and you decide at onboarding whether buyers or you pay the fee.

Associations, companies, schools and private individuals can register, and each organisation is checked and approved before sales start. Buyers check out as guests and pay by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay where supported, Klarna, SEPA direct debit or Bancontact. Tickets arrive as a PDF with a QR code. Helpers scan with shareable browser links on their phones, with internet. The payout reaches your IBAN by the 7th bank working day after the event.

What Eventoren doesn't offer: Eventbrite-style marketplace reach, seat maps, a waitlist, a native app, discount codes, PayPal, iDEAL or more than one currency. The interface is German and the organiser contract is under German law, so it suits euro-area organisers, especially in DACH. If those limits matter, one of the alternatives above is the better fit. ## Frequently asked questions

### Who owns Eventbrite in 2026?

Bending Spoons, a software company headquartered in Milan, has owned Eventbrite since 10 March 2026. It paid $4.50 per share in cash, about $500 M in total, and Eventbrite was delisted from the New York Stock Exchange.

### What is the best Eventbrite alternative in Europe?

There's no single winner. On cost for tickets up to €50, Eventfrog Free and Weezevent lead. Weeztix suits the Netherlands and Belgium, EVENTIM.Light and pretix suit seated events in Germany, and Billetto suits the Nordics. Match the platform to your country, ticket price and door needs.

### Is there a free Eventbrite alternative?

For free events, Eventbrite, Eventoren, Eventfrog and Weezevent charge nothing. Ticket Tailor and Weeztix are free up to 5,000 free tickets a year, and pretix up to 500 plus one per paid ticket. For paid tickets, payment processing always costs something.

### What is Eventbrite Premium?

Premium replaces the Pro plan for new subscribers. It costs €59 per month or €588 per year in Germany, France, Ireland, Italy and Spain, and includes up to 2,000 marketing emails per day, a $20 monthly ads credit and 50 % off instant payouts. The rollout is gradual, starting in the US.

### Can I run a box office with Eventbrite in the euro area?

Not in the organiser app: Eventbrite's in-app door sales support only AUD, CAD, GBP and USD events. For EUR door sales, use a platform with a box office, such as EVENTIM.Light, pretix or Weezevent, or a cashless setup like the one in our guide to [box office and door sales](/en/guides/box-office-door-sales).
