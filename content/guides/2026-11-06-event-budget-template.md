An event budget template lists every cost line with a planned and an actual figure, splits costs into fixed and variable, adds a contingency, sets realistic income against it and shows when money leaves and arrives. The last step is the one most templates skip: venues, bands and technicians want deposits weeks ahead, while ticket money often arrives only after the event. Below you get a copy-ready template, a contingency rule, a full worked example in EUR for a 400-guest party with break-even and cash flow, and a post-event review table (as of November 2026).

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Split costs into fixed (due whatever happens) and variable (rising with each guest). Without that split you cannot calculate break-even.
> - Our rule of thumb for contingency: 10 % of fixed costs for formats you know, 15 % for a first event, 15 to 20 % outdoors. It is a planning rule, not an industry statistic.
> - Plan cash flow, not just profit. In our 400-guest example the organiser has to pre-finance up to about €3,750 although the event ends €3,121 in profit.
> - With Eventoren, ticket income is paid out by the 7th bank working day after the event at the latest, so deposits must come from your own funds or sponsors.
> - After the event, compare plan and actual line by line within two to four weeks. That review is the first draft of your next budget.

## What is an event budget, and what goes into it?

**Definition:** An **event budget** is the planned comparison of all costs and income of one event, broken into lines, each with a planned value and, after the event, an actual value. It answers three questions: what does the event cost, how many guests do I need to cover it, and when do I need how much money in the account?

Two terms do most of the work:

- **Fixed costs** are due whether 150 or 400 people come: venue, artist fees, tech, insurance, marketing, core staff. Some music licences are fixed too, for example tariffs based on room size and entry price.
- **Variable costs** rise with each ticket or guest: ticketing fees, bar stock, wristbands, included drinks, and music licences calculated as a percentage of box office (common for concerts).

To derive ticket prices from this, see [how to price event tickets](/en/guides/how-to-price-event-tickets). This guide goes one step further: from ticket price to a complete budget with several income sources, a contingency, payment dates and a review.

Three rules make the template robust:

1. **Every line has a source:** a quote, last year's invoice or a published tariff. Mark estimates visibly so they get replaced.
2. **Gross or net, but consistent.** If you cannot reclaim VAT (small or exempt organisations), budget all costs gross. If you can, budget net and deduct VAT from ticket income too. Mixing creates phantom profit.
3. **No "miscellaneous" line above 5 %.** Anything larger gets its own line.

## How big should your contingency be?

**Definition:** A **contingency** (buffer, reserve) is a fixed amount in the budget not assigned to any line. It absorbs costs you cannot know at planning time: an extra steward demanded by the authority, a generator, higher cleaning bills, a replacement DJ.

| Situation                                               | Contingency on fixed costs |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Format running for years, all partners known            | 10 %                       |
| First event of its kind or new venue                    | 15 %                       |
| Outdoor, weather-dependent tech, open permit conditions | 15–20 %                    |

Two things matter more than the exact percentage. First, the contingency belongs in the break-even calculation; a profit planned without it is the first thing a surprise eats. Second, decide who may release it, for example only the treasurer and chair together. If it stays untouched, it becomes part of the surplus.

## Which income should you plan with?

Most budgets fail on optimistic income, not on costs. Plan every source conservatively and calculate scenarios.

| Income source          | How to plan                                         | Typical mistake                  |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Presale tickets        | Allocation per ticket type, after ticketing fees    | Full capacity as the base case   |
| Door sales             | Small allocation, full only in the best case        | Counting on walk-ups             |
| Sponsorship            | Only amounts confirmed in writing                   | Counting verbal promises         |
| Bar and food           | Contribution (sales minus cost of goods), not sales | Treating sales as profit         |
| Cloakroom, merchandise | Only with past figures                              | Forgetting unsold stock          |
| Grants                 | Only after the decision letter                      | Treating an application as money |

If you absorb the ticketing fee, it is a variable cost. If you pass it on, the price buyers see rises, and EU rules require the total price including unavoidable fees from the first price shown.

## Worked example: budget for a 400-guest party

**Worked example:** a sports club in Belgium runs a summer party with a live band on Saturday 12 June 2027 in a hall for up to 400 guests. The club is not VAT-registered, so every amount is gross. All values are our assumptions; replace them with your quotes.

### Fixed costs

| Line                                 | Basis                                 | Planned    |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Hall incl. cleaning                  | quote                                 | €1,800     |
| Sound, light, stage incl. technician | quote                                 | €1,400     |
| Band (self-employed musicians)       | flat fee                              | €2,200     |
| Music licence                        | estimate, check your society's tariff | €600       |
| Security                             | quote                                 | €1,100     |
| First aid                            | quote                                 | €300       |
| 4 staff × 6 h × €15                  | assumption                            | €360       |
| Event liability insurance            | single-event policy                   | €150       |
| Posters, flyers, social ads          | plan                                  | €650       |
| Decoration, wristbands, materials    | plan                                  | €300       |
| Permits and fees                     | estimate                              | €150       |
| **Fixed costs before contingency**   |                                       | **€9,010** |
| Contingency 10 %                     | format runs every year                | €901       |
| **Fixed costs with contingency**     |                                       | **€9,911** |

### Income and variable costs

| Source          | Quantity × price  | Gross       | Variable costs              | Contribution   |
| --------------- | ----------------- | ----------- | --------------------------- | -------------- |
| Early bird      | 100 × €16         | €1,600      | Fee €1.11 × 100 = €111.00   | €1,489.00      |
| Regular         | 220 × €20         | €4,400      | Fee €1.27 × 220 = €279.40   | €4,120.60      |
| Door (cashless) | 80 × €24          | €1,920      | Fee €1.43 × 80 = €114.40    | €1,805.60      |
| Bar             | 400 guests × €12  | €4,800      | Cost of goods 33 % = €1,584 | €3,216.00      |
| Sponsorship     | €1,000 + 2 × €250 | €1,500      | –                           | €1,500.00      |
| **Total**       |                   | **€14,220** |                             | **€12,131.20** |

The ticketing fee is Eventoren's 3.9 % of the gross price plus €0.49, absorbed by the club and rounded to the cent. Bar spend per head and cost of goods are assumptions; use figures from your last event.

### Result and scenarios

| Scenario | Guests (early / regular / door) | Contribution | Result before contingency | Result with contingency spent |
| -------- | ------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Plan     | 400 (100 / 220 / 80)            | €12,131.20   | +€3,121.20                | +€2,220.20                    |
| Medium   | 320 (100 / 180 / 40)            | €9,836.00    | +€826.00                  | −€75.00                       |
| Weak     | 240 (100 / 120 / 20)            | €7,617.60    | −€1,392.40                | −€2,293.40                    |

**Break-even:** after the 100 early bird guests (contribution €2,293 including their bar spend) and sponsorship, each extra regular guest brings €18.73 from the ticket plus €8.04 bar contribution, so €26.77. Without contingency: 100 + (€9,010 − €1,500 − €2,293) ÷ €26.77 = 295 guests (74 % of capacity). With the contingency spent: 329 guests (82 %).

The lesson: the party works, but narrowly. The biggest levers are the band fee (a door deal instead of a flat fee), one more sponsor and a higher early bird price.

## Cash flow: when does money move?

A budget can end in profit and still go negative on the way. Venue and band want deposits, drinks are bought before the event, and many ticketing platforms pay out only after it. With Eventoren the payout arrives by bank transfer by the 7th bank working day after the event at the latest. For the party on Saturday 12 June 2027 that is Tuesday 22 June 2027 at the latest (no Belgian public holiday falls in between).

| When            | What                                                    | Amount            | Running balance |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | --------------- |
| 6 months before | Hall deposit 50 %                                       | −€900             | −€900           |
| 4 months before | Band deposit 50 %; main sponsor pays on signing         | −€1,100 / +€1,000 | −€1,000         |
| 3 months before | Insurance, permits                                      | −€300             | −€1,300         |
| 8 weeks before  | Print and ads                                           | −€650             | −€1,950         |
| 4 weeks before  | Tech deposit 30 %; two partner sponsors pay             | −€420 / +€500     | −€1,870         |
| Event day       | Drinks stock, decoration and materials                  | −€1,884           | **−€3,754**     |
| Event night     | Bar takings                                             | +€4,800           | +€1,046         |
| Week after      | Rest of hall, tech and band; security, first aid, staff | −€4,740           | −€3,694         |
| By 22 June 2027 | Ticket payout after fees                                | +€7,415.20        | +€3,721.20      |
| 4–8 weeks after | Music licence invoice                                   | −€600             | **+€3,121.20**  |

The club therefore needs about **€3,750** of its own money on the event day, even in the plan scenario. With fewer guests, the low point after the event gets deeper, because the bills stay the same. How to ease it:

![Busy bar on event night: bartenders serving drinks to laughing guests in warm light](ratgeber-eventbudget-planen-szene-2)

- **Negotiate payment terms:** final payments for hall, tech and security 14 days after the event instead of on the night.
- **Collect sponsors early:** payment on signing or at least four weeks before.
- **Drinks on sale-or-return:** return unopened stock, pay only for what was sold.
- **Approve a float:** the board releases a fixed amount from reserves, repaid after the payout.
- **Know your cancellation exposure:** if you cancel, you refund tickets while deposits are often lost. See [event cancellation and refunds in the EU](/en/guides/event-cancellation-refunds-eu).

## How do you keep the budget alive during planning?

- [ ] One person (treasurer, finance lead) owns the file; everyone else reports spending before committing
- [ ] Sign nothing above €100 before checking it against the budget
- [ ] Quotes, contracts and invoices in one shared folder, named after the budget line
- [ ] Update presale figures every two weeks and pick the matching scenario
- [ ] Four weeks out at the latest: cut costs, raise promotion or switch to plan B
- [ ] Log every contingency release: who, when, what for
- [ ] Hand over cash takings with a count sheet signed by two people

Keep receipts for your national retention period; in Germany, accounting vouchers are kept for 8 years since 2025.

## Post-event review: how do you evaluate the budget?

**Definition:** A **post-event review** (plan-versus-actual comparison) sets each planned line against the actual figure and explains the gap. Do it two to four weeks after the event while memories are fresh, and enter missing invoices as "expected".

| Line             | Planned | Actual | Variance | Cause                               | Next time                    |
| ---------------- | ------- | ------ | -------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Security         | €1,100  | €1,265 | +15 %    | Authority required one more steward | Ask about conditions earlier |
| Bar contribution | €3,216  | €2,730 | −15 %    | Heat: more water, fewer cocktails   | Adjust water price and range |
| Door tickets     | 80      | 45     | −44 %    | Rain from 22:00                     | Don't count on the door      |
| …                |         |        |          |                                     |                              |

The actual figures above are illustrative. Track four KPIs from event to event:

- **Cost per guest:** fixed costs ÷ actual guests. Shows whether the format gets more efficient.
- **Contribution per guest:** ticket after fees plus bar contribution. Shows whether prices and range fit.
- **Presale share of ticket revenue:** the higher, the more predictable. See [event planning checklist](/en/guides/event-planning-checklist) for when to start selling.
- **No-show rate:** (tickets sold − check-ins) ÷ tickets sold. Matters for bar and staff planning.

Finish with three sentences: what cost more than planned, which income was too optimistic, what changes next time. They are the first lines of your next budget.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I create an event budget?

List every cost line with planned values from real quotes and split them into fixed and variable. Add a contingency of 10 to 15 % of fixed costs and set conservative income against it. Calculate at least three scenarios and plan when each payment is due.

### How much contingency should an event budget have?

We recommend 10 % of fixed costs for formats you know and 15 % for first events or new venues; outdoor events with weather risk can justify 20 %. That is a planning rule, not a statistically proven rate. Include the contingency in your break-even calculation.

### When do I receive the money from ticket sales?

It depends on the platform; many pay out after the event. With Eventoren, the payout arrives by bank transfer by the 7th bank working day after the event at the latest. You therefore need to pre-finance deposits for venue, artists and tech.

### What is the difference between fixed and variable event costs?

Fixed costs are due regardless of attendance, such as venue hire, artist fees and tech. Variable costs rise with each guest, such as ticketing fees and bar stock. Only with this split can you calculate how many guests you need to break even.
