To run a club night as an independent promoter, you hire or partner with a club, book DJs, sell tickets in advance and at the door, and manage the guest list so it does not eat your income. Whether you make or lose money depends less on the DJ than on the deal with the club: a flat hire, a door split, a bar share or a bar guarantee spread the risk in completely different ways.

This guide compares all four on the same 350-capacity night, for a good and a weak turnout. It then covers DJ fees and artist tax, the presale vs door mix, guest list discipline, promoter teams and the KPIs that turn one night into a series (as of November 2026).

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Four core deals: flat hire (you carry the risk, the club keeps the bar), door split (shared risk), bar share (upside for you) and bar guarantee (high risk, high reward). A fifth, the club paying you a fee, only comes once your brand pulls a crowd.
> - In our worked example, a weak night costs the promoter between €668 and €1,408 under the four risk-bearing deals. A door split loses least.
> - Foreign DJs can trigger withholding tax where they play: in Germany 15.825 % on fees above €250 per performance. Agree whether a fee is gross or net.
> - Cap the guest list (we use about 10 % of capacity), give each promoter a quota and a cut-off time, and measure attendance per list after every night.
> - Sell most tickets in advance: a visible €5 gap between presale and door price rewards early buyers and tells you two weeks out whether to push.

This guide is not legal or tax advice.

## What is a promoter-run club night?

**Definition:** A promoter-run club night (in German _Fremdveranstaltung_, in Dutch often _inhuur_) is a night where an external promoter, collective or association takes over a club. The club provides room, bar, sound system and usually security; the promoter provides line-up, audience and ticket sales. Unless the contract says otherwise, the promoter is the organiser for music licensing, youth protection and ticket sales.

Agree in writing who covers what:

| Task                       | Usually the club             | Usually the promoter        | Clarify in the contract     |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Room, bar, alcohol licence | yes                          |                             | Opening hours, curfew       |
| Sound and lights           | yes                          | Extra gear                  | Is a technician included?   |
| Security, cloakroom        | often                        |                             | Headcount, who pays         |
| Door and box office        | shared                       | Ticket scanning, guest list | Who counts the door?        |
| Line-up, fees, travel      |                              | yes                         | Hotel, rider                |
| Promotion                  | Club channels                | yes                         | Minimum effort, approvals   |
| Music licence              | often the club's own licence | otherwise you               | Get it confirmed in writing |

### Music licensing

Public club nights need a music licence from the national collecting society. Examples: GEMA (Germany), AKM (Austria), SACEM (France), Buma/Stemra and Sena (Netherlands), Unisono (Belgium), IMRO (Ireland), PPL PRS (UK), SIAE (Italy) and SGAE (Spain). Many clubs hold a licence that also covers external promoters; others expect you to register the night yourself before it happens. Ask, and get the answer in writing. In Germany, a DJ night with an entry fee usually falls under tariff M-V, calculated from room size and the highest entry price.

## Which deals do clubs offer promoters?

| Deal              | How it works                                                                       | Who carries the risk   | Fits when                                |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Flat hire**     | You pay a fixed rent and keep the door; the club keeps the bar                     | You                    | You know your crowd will come            |
| **Door split**    | Door income is split by ratio, e.g. 70/30 in your favour                           | Shared                 | New series, club believes in the concept |
| **Bar share**     | On top of hire or split you get a percentage of bar sales, often above a threshold | Shared, upside for you | Your crowd drinks well                   |
| **Bar guarantee** | No rent, but you guarantee the club minimum bar sales and pay any shortfall        | You, very high         | Full rooms are certain                   |
| **Promoter fee**  | The club pays you a fixed sum; door and bar go to the club                         | Club                   | Your brand is established                |

Clubs often combine these: low hire plus a bar share, or a "break" where the club takes the door until its costs are covered and the rest is split.

Three details decide whether a deal is fair:

1. **Base of the split.** Is the door split on gross or net income, before or after VAT and ticketing fees? At 19 % VAT that is a big difference.
2. **Guest list.** Do guest-list visitors count towards bar thresholds and capacity? How many places does the club keep for itself?
3. **Transparency.** Who counts the door, and who sees the bar report? Without access to bar figures, a bar share runs on trust.

## Worked example: five deals, one night

**Worked example:** a collective runs a night in 2027 in a German club with 350 capacity. Presale €15, door €20. The collective is VAT-registered and charges 19 % VAT (party nights in Germany are usually 19 %; 7 % can apply when the DJ performance itself is the reason to attend). It absorbs the Eventoren fee of 3.9 % + €0.49 per ticket (€1.08 on €15, €1.27 on €20). Fees and deals are our assumptions.

### Promoter costs (same in every deal)

| Line                                                     | Amount        |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- |
| Headliner DJ from abroad, €700 agreed net of tax         | €700.00       |
| German withholding gross-up: €700 ÷ (1 − 0.15825) − €700 | €131.60       |
| Two local DJs at €150                                    | €300.00       |
| Headliner travel and hotel                               | €250.00       |
| Social ads and posters                                   | €350.00       |
| Promoter commissions                                     | €150.00       |
| **Total**                                                | **€1,881.60** |

Not included: the German artists' social levy (5.0 % of artist fees in 2027), which a collective that mainly runs nights may owe. Add it if it applies to you.

### Good night: 300 paying guests

230 presale × €15 + 70 door × €20 = €4,850 gross. After 19 % VAT (€774.37) and ticketing fees (€337.30), **net door is €3,738.33**. With 30 guest-list places, 330 people are inside, spending €14 each at the bar on average: €4,620 gross, €3,882.35 net.

| Deal                                   | Calculation                          | Promoter result |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------- |
| A: Flat hire €900, bar to club         | 3,738.33 − 900 − 1,881.60            | **+€956.73**    |
| B: Door split 70/30 of net door        | 0.7 × 3,738.33 − 1,881.60            | **+€735.23**    |
| C: Hire €1,100 + 10 % of net bar       | 3,738.33 − 1,100 + 388.24 − 1,881.60 | **+€1,144.97**  |
| D: No hire, €3,500 gross bar guarantee | bar reached, no shortfall            | **+€1,856.73**  |
| E: Club pays promoter fee €2,200       | 2,200 − 1,881.60                     | **+€318.40**    |

### Weak night: 140 paying guests

110 presale and 30 door: €2,250 gross, **€1,733.86 net door**. With 20 guest-list places, 160 people are inside; the bar takes €2,240 gross (€1,882.35 net).

| Deal                               | Calculation                          | Promoter result |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------- |
| A: Flat hire                       | 1,733.86 − 900 − 1,881.60            | **−€1,047.74**  |
| B: Door split 70/30                | 0.7 × 1,733.86 − 1,881.60            | **−€667.90**    |
| C: Hire + 10 % bar                 | 1,733.86 − 1,100 + 188.24 − 1,881.60 | **−€1,059.50**  |
| D: Bar guarantee, €1,260 shortfall | 1,733.86 − 1,260 − 1,881.60          | **−€1,407.74**  |
| E: Promoter fee                    | unchanged                            | **+€318.40**    |

### What the numbers show

- **The door split** is the safest choice for a new series: the smallest loss on a weak night, a solid result on a good one.
- **Bar guarantees** pay best when you fill the room and hurt most when you don't. On the weak night you pay for empty bars.
- **A bar share** needs high bar spend per head. At €14 it beats flat hire on a good night but not on a weak one.
- **A promoter fee** removes your risk and your upside. It is the deal to aim for after a season of strong numbers.

## What do DJs cost, and what about artist tax?

DJ fees depend on draw, city and weekday; there is no reliable public market data. Agree these points as well as the fee:

| Item                    | What to agree                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fee                     | Flat, door deal, or "guarantee versus percentage" (the higher amount is paid) |
| Gross or net            | Whether withholding tax comes out of the fee or on top                        |
| Travel, hotel, catering | Who books and pays, written into the rider                                    |
| Tech                    | Players and mixer per rider, booth monitor                                    |
| Set time                | Slot, handovers, closing set                                                  |
| Guest list              | Fixed number, e.g. +2                                                         |
| Cancellation            | Rules for both sides                                                          |

### Foreign DJs and withholding tax

Many European countries tax non-resident performers where they play, with the organiser withholding the tax from the fee. In Germany it is 15 % plus 5.5 % solidarity surcharge on that tax, 15.825 % in total (§ 50a EStG). No tax is withheld if income per performance does not exceed €250. Double tax treaties and other countries' rules differ, so check with the tax office where the club is. If you agree a "net" fee, you pay the gross-up, as the example shows.

### Social levies

Some countries add levies on artist fees. In Germany the artists' social levy (KSA) is 4.9 % in 2026 and 5.0 % in 2027 on fees paid to self-employed artists, whether they live in Germany or not. A collective whose main purpose is putting on nights may owe it from the first euro.

## Presale or door: what mix should you aim for?

Club crowds decide late, but steering the presale still pays off:

- **Planning:** two weeks out, presale tells you whether to push ads, trim the guest list or adjust staff.
- **Price incentive:** a visible gap (€5 in the example) rewards early buyers. Publish the door price from day one.
- **Faster entry:** presale guests are only scanned; door buyers take longer.

| Ticket type | Allocation | Price | Purpose                     |
| ----------- | ---------- | ----- | --------------------------- |
| Early bird  | 50         | €12   | First momentum              |
| Presale     | 200        | €15   | Main allocation             |
| Door        | max. 70    | €20   | Walk-ups                    |
| Guest list  | max. 30    | €0    | DJs, promoters, press, crew |
| **Total**   | **350**    |       | never above capacity        |

Aim for presale plus bar to cover your costs; treat the door as upside. How a cashless door works is covered in [box office and door sales](/en/guides/box-office-door-sales), and tier design in [early bird tickets](/en/guides/early-bird-tickets).

## How do you keep the guest list under control?

The guest list is the biggest leak in club economics: every name costs you a ticket and still counts towards capacity.

- [ ] Set a total cap before announcing, e.g. 10 % of capacity
- [ ] Fixed quotas per person: each DJ, each promoter, the club, press, crew
- [ ] Plus-ones count against the quota
- [ ] Deadline for names on the afternoon of the night
- [ ] Cut-off time at the door, e.g. guest list until 00:30
- [ ] After each night: names entered vs checked in, per quota
- [ ] Promoters whose lists don't show get fewer places next time

A full template is in [guest list management](/en/guides/guest-list-management).

## Which promoter models work?

| Model                 | Pay                              | Advantage                | Risk                           |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Guest-list promoter   | Free places for their friends    | Costs no cash            | Fills the room, not the till   |
| Commission per ticket | Fixed amount per ticket sold     | Pays only for results    | Needs clean attribution        |
| Revenue share team    | Percentage of net door           | Strong incentive         | Settlement must be transparent |
| Ambassador            | Free ticket after X tickets sold | Works well with students | Hard to plan                   |

To attribute sales, give each promoter something trackable: a dedicated ticket type with its own allocation, or an exclusive allocation unlocked by codes. Commission is taxable income for the promoter; if you pay regularly, check with an adviser how to engage them in your country.

## How do you turn one night into a series?

A series pays because promotion, audience and the relationship with the club compound from night to night.

- **Fixed rhythm:** same weekday, same week of the month, so people plan around you.
- **Recognisable brand:** name, visuals and residents who play every time.
- **Own channels:** Instagram, a WhatsApp or Telegram channel, and an email list built with consent (under GDPR and ePrivacy, past buyers can get similar offers with a clear opt-out).
- **Negotiating power:** after three or four solid nights, bring your numbers and ask for a better split.
- **Tax:** a regular series is a business activity almost everywhere. Register, charge VAT where required and watch small-business thresholds.

### KPIs after every night

| KPI                | Formula                         | Use                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Utilisation        | guests ÷ capacity               | Room choice, deal talks |
| Presale share      | presale tickets ÷ paying guests | Planning certainty      |
| Guest list share   | guest-list guests ÷ all guests  | Spot the leak           |
| Show rate          | scanned tickets ÷ sold tickets  | Capacity planning       |
| Bar per head       | bar sales ÷ guests              | Value of bar deals      |
| Ad cost per ticket | ad spend ÷ tickets sold         | Channel choice          |
| Result per guest   | result ÷ guests                 | Compare deals           |

In the example, the good night has 94 % utilisation, a 77 % presale share and a 9 % guest list share. The weak night drops to 46 % utilisation while the guest list share rises to 13 %, which is the classic warning sign.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best club deal for a new night?

Usually a door split, because the club shares the risk. In our example it loses the least on a weak night and still earns well on a good one. Flat hire and bar guarantees pay off once you reliably fill the room.

### Who needs the music licence for a club night?

The organiser, usually the promoter. If the club's licence covers external promoters, you may not need your own. Get that confirmed in writing; otherwise register the night with the national collecting society before it takes place.

### Do I need to withhold tax for foreign DJs?

Often yes, in the country where the night takes place. In Germany you withhold 15.825 % in total when the fee per performance exceeds €250 and pay it to the Federal Central Tax Office. Other countries have their own rules, so check locally.

### How big should the guest list be?

There is no fixed rule. We plan with at most about 10 % of capacity, split into fixed quotas. After a few nights, your own show rates tell you whether that fits. Tickets sold plus guest list must never exceed the licensed capacity.

### How many tickets should I sell in advance?

As many as possible, but plan so that presale plus bar covers your costs. In our example, about three quarters of paying guests bought in advance on the good night. Track the presale share per night and adjust prices and promotion.
