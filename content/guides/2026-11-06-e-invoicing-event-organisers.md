E-invoicing means sending invoices as structured data that software can read, not as a PDF or on paper. For most event organisers, it does **not** change how you sell tickets to the public: consumer (B2C) sales are outside most national mandates. It does change how you invoice **businesses**, such as sponsors, exhibitors, traders and companies booking group tickets, and how you **receive** invoices from venues, production companies and caterers. Several EU countries already require B2B e-invoices, more follow in 2027 and 2028, and the EU's **ViDA** package makes e-invoicing mandatory for cross-border B2B supplies from **1 July 2030**. Here's the timeline country by country, as of November 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - ViDA (adopted 11 March 2025): Member States may impose domestic e-invoicing now; cross-border B2B e-invoicing and digital reporting start 1 July 2030.
> - Already live: Italy (since 2019), Belgium (since 1 January 2026), Poland (KSeF since February/April 2026), France (receiving since 1 September 2026).
> - Next: Germany issues from 2027 (turnover above €800,000) or 2028 (everyone else); France SMEs 1 September 2027; Spain 6 October 2027 / 6 October 2028.
> - Ticket sales to consumers are generally out of scope. Germany also exempts invoices up to €250 gross.
> - Sponsor invoices, stand fees and company group bookings are where organisers are hit, plus every incoming invoice from suppliers.

This guide is not legal or tax advice.

## What is an e-invoice?

**An e-invoice is an invoice issued, transmitted and received in a structured electronic format that allows automatic processing.** The EU standard for its content is **EN 16931**. Common formats include XRechnung and ZUGFeRD in Germany, Factur-X in France, invoices over the **Peppol** network in Belgium, and the formats of the state platforms in Italy (SdI) and Poland (KSeF).

The key point many organisers miss: a normal PDF sent by email is **not** an e-invoice. It is a picture of an invoice that a human has to retype. In Germany, it has been classed as an "other invoice" since 2025.

| Term                               | Meaning                                                                     | Example                                                                                 |

## What does ViDA change, and when?

**ViDA** (VAT in the Digital Age) is a package of three EU acts: Council Directive (EU) 2025/516, Regulation 2025/517 and Implementing Regulation 2025/518. It was adopted on **11 March 2025**, entered into force on 14 April 2025, and is being rolled out until January 2035. According to the European Commission:

| Date                                | What happens                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Since entry into force (April 2025) | Member States can introduce mandatory domestic e-invoicing under certain conditions, without asking for an EU derogation              |
| 1 January 2027                      | Minor clarifications for the OSS and IOSS schemes                                                                                     |
| 1 July 2028                         | Deemed-supplier rule for short-term accommodation and passenger transport platforms (not ticketing); Single VAT Registration measures |
| **1 July 2030**                     | Digital reporting requirements based on e-invoicing; main impact on businesses making **cross-border B2B** supplies                   |
| 1 January 2035                      | Existing national real-time reporting systems must be aligned with the EU model                                                       |

For an organiser, the 2030 date matters if you invoice businesses in other EU countries: a Dutch sponsor of your German festival, or an Austrian company booking a conference package. Until then, national rules decide.

## Which countries already require B2B e-invoicing?

As of November 2026. Dates come from national tax authority pages where we could fetch them; we flag the rest.

| Country     | Status                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Who                                                       |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Italy**   | Mandatory via the SdI exchange system since 1 January 2019, B2B and B2C; flat-rate taxpayers since 2024                                                                                                                          | Practically all VAT-registered businesses                 |
| **Belgium** | Structured B2B e-invoices mandatory since **1 January 2026**, typically over Peppol; a tolerance period ran until 31 March 2026; e-reporting planned for 2028 (draft)                                                            | VAT-registered Belgian businesses dealing with each other |
| **Poland**  | KSeF mandatory from **1 February 2026** for the largest taxpayers (sales above PLN 200 million in 2024) and from **1 April 2026** for others; small-seller relief (up to PLN 10,000 a month) ends 1 January 2027                 | VAT taxpayers                                             |
| **France**  | Since **1 September 2026**, all businesses must be able to **receive** e-invoices through an approved platform; large and mid-sized companies also issue and e-report; SMEs and micro-businesses issue from **1 September 2027** | VAT-liable businesses established in France               |
| **Germany** | **Receive** since 1 January 2025 (an email inbox is enough); **issue** from 1 January 2027 if 2026 turnover exceeded €800,000; from **1 January 2028** for everyone except small businesses under § 19 UStG                      | Domestic B2B                                              |
| **Spain**   | Royal Decree 238/2026 and Order HAC/1028/2026 (in force 6 October 2026) start the clock: issuing and receiving from **6 October 2027** for businesses with turnover above €8 million, from **6 October 2028** for all others     | Domestic B2B between businesses established in Spain      |

Two points on Germany, as of 7 November 2026: there is **no postponement**. A trade association asked in June 2026 to push the 2027 stage back, but no bill followed, and the finance ministry's guidance of 15 October 2026 added detail without changing dates. Check again before 1 January 2027. In Spain, the Verifactu anti-fraud invoicing software rules are a separate regime; we don't cover their dates here.

## Do ticket sales need e-invoices?

Mostly no. National B2B mandates target invoices between businesses. Selling a ticket to a private person is B2C, and B2C is outside the German, French, Belgian, Polish and Spanish B2B mandates. Italy is the exception, because its SdI system also covers B2C invoices; check with your Italian adviser if you issue invoices there.

Germany has an additional exemption that matters for tickets: **invoices up to €250 gross** (small-amount invoices, § 33 UStDV) can always be sent as an ordinary invoice, even to businesses. Note that the limit applies **per invoice**, not per ticket.

| Sale                                                       | E-invoice in Germany?                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Private buyer, any amount                                  | No (B2C)                                                                           |
| A company buys one conference ticket for €199              | No (up to €250)                                                                    |
| A company buys 10 tickets at €30 on one invoice = €300     | **Yes**, from 2027 or 2028                                                         |
| A company buys 15 tickets at the door and pays on the spot | Yes; the German FAQ allows a receipt on site and a corrective e-invoice afterwards |

Other countries have their own thresholds and exemptions. Before you invoice a business in another country, check which rules apply: where you and your customer are established decides which mandate is triggered.

## What about sponsors, stand fees and venue invoices?

This is where e-invoicing actually reaches organisers. Look at who pays you and whom you pay.

| Invoice                                         | Direction | Typical amount   | Affected?                                         |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Sponsorship (advertising in return for payment) | outgoing  | usually high     | **Yes**, once your country's issuing date applies |
| Stand fee for traders, food trucks, exhibitors  | outgoing  | often above €250 | **Yes**                                           |
| Group booking or corporate package              | outgoing  | above €250       | **Yes**                                           |
| Venue hire, tent rental                         | incoming  | high             | You must be able to **receive**                   |
| Sound, light, security, catering, printing      | incoming  | high             | You must be able to **receive**                   |
| Artist fee via an agency (company)              | incoming  | high             | You must be able to **receive**                   |

**Example (Germany, as of November 2026):** a VAT-registered cultural association had €180,000 total turnover in 2026 and runs a town festival in 2027.

- A private visitor buys 4 tickets at €18 = €72: no e-invoice (B2C).
- A car dealership buys 25 tickets at €18 = €450 for its staff: e-invoice needed, but only for supplies from 2028, because the association was below €800,000 in 2026. In 2027, paper or PDF (with the customer's agreement) still works.
- A brewery sponsors for €1,500 net + 19 % VAT = €1,785: same logic, ordinary invoice in 2027, e-invoice from 2028.
- The town hall sends its rental invoice as XRechnung in 2027: the association has had to accept that since 2025 and must archive the structured file unchanged.

Had the organiser turned over more than €800,000 in 2026, say a festival company, the invoices to the dealership and brewery would need to be e-invoices from **1 January 2027**.

### Clubs and small businesses

In Germany, a club is affected only where it acts as a business: events with admission, catering, sponsorship with advertising, stand rental. Membership fees are not. Small businesses under the German small-business scheme don't have to issue e-invoices, but must be able to receive them. France and Spain extend their mandates to small businesses with later dates. Ask your adviser how your entity type is treated.

### Which format should you choose?

For mixed customers, a **hybrid** format (ZUGFeRD in Germany, Factur-X in France) is often the easiest: it looks like a normal PDF to a person and carries XML for software. In Belgium, Peppol is the default route. In Poland and Italy, invoices go through the state platform, so your accounting software must connect to KSeF or SdI. In France, you need an approved platform (plateforme agréée).

## How to receive and check e-invoices

1. **Set up a dedicated address or access point**, for example invoices@yourclub.org, or a Peppol ID in Belgium, and send it to all suppliers.
2. **Use a viewer or software.** Raw XML isn't readable. German tax authorities offer a free viewer for XRechnung; many accounting tools import the common formats.
3. **Archive the original file unchanged** for your national retention period. A printout isn't enough. In Germany, invoices are kept for 8 years.
4. **Insist on the correct format** from suppliers who are obliged to send one. An invalid invoice can put your input VAT deduction at risk.

## E-invoicing checklist for event organisers

**Now**

- [ ] Find out which mandate applies where you are established and where your business customers are.
- [ ] Make sure you can receive e-invoices (inbox, Peppol ID, platform or KSeF access).
- [ ] Archive structured invoices unchanged for the full retention period.
- [ ] Decide who checks incoming invoices (treasurer, accountant).

**Before your issuing date**

- [ ] List all outgoing B2B invoices: sponsors, stand fees, group bookings, rentals.
- [ ] Check last year's turnover against your country's threshold (DE €800,000; ES €8 million).
- [ ] Check whether you qualify for a small-business exemption.
- [ ] Add billing address, VAT number and preferred format to 2027 sponsor contracts.
- [ ] Switch your invoicing software to a compliant format and send a test invoice.
- [ ] Agree who issues the invoice for company group bookings: you or your ticketing platform.

**Cross-border**

- [ ] Note which sponsors and exhibitors are in other EU countries.
- [ ] Plan for ViDA cross-border B2B e-invoicing from 1 July 2030.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need e-invoices for event tickets?

Not for tickets sold to private buyers, because B2C is outside most national B2B mandates (Italy also covers B2C). For company buyers, it depends on your country. In Germany, invoices up to €250 gross are exempt, but a company booking above that needs an e-invoice from 2027 or 2028.

### When does ViDA make e-invoicing mandatory?

For cross-border B2B supplies within the EU from 1 July 2030, combined with digital reporting. Since April 2025, Member States have also been able to impose domestic e-invoicing without an EU derogation, which is why national dates come first.

### Is a PDF invoice an e-invoice?

No. A plain PDF is unstructured. A hybrid PDF with embedded structured data, such as ZUGFeRD or Factur-X, can be an e-invoice if the data meets EN 16931 or the national format.

### When does Germany's e-invoicing mandate start?

All domestic businesses must be able to receive e-invoices since 1 January 2025. Issuing becomes mandatory from 1 January 2027 if 2026 turnover exceeded €800,000, and from 1 January 2028 for everyone else except small businesses. As of 7 November 2026, no postponement has been decided.

### Does my club need to issue e-invoices?

Only for its business activity, such as sponsorship, stand fees or company ticket bookings, and only once your country's issuing date applies to you. Membership fees are not affected. Check whether a small-business exemption applies.
