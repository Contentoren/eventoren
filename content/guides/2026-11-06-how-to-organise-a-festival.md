To organise a festival, you secure a site and date 12 to 18 months ahead, build a budget with a break-even point, agree the permit route with your local authority, book a headliner who sells your first tickets, and run production, entry and safety to a written plan. For a first festival of 1,500 visitors a day, the real risk is a budget that only works at 100 % sales: in our worked example, break-even sits at about 86 % of planned income once a 10 % contingency is included. Below: the 2027 timeline, the full EUR budget, permits, artist tax, ticket tiers and a checklist, as of November 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Allow 12 to 18 months. If you start in November 2026 for late August 2027, you have about nine months, so lock site, permit route and headliner within eight weeks.
> - Worked example, 1,500 visitors over two days: about €160,600 net income against €142,900 costs including a 10 % contingency. Break-even sits at roughly 86 % of planned sales.
> - Expect four permit areas almost everywhere in Europe: temporary structures, a safety and crowd plan, noise, and medical cover, plus an alcohol licence if you run the bar.
> - Foreign performers often trigger withholding tax where the show takes place. In Germany it is 15 % plus solidarity surcharge, 15.825 % in total, for fees above €250 per performance.
> - Sell day, weekend and camping as separate ticket types with their own capacities, and run early bird as a small, limited ticket type.

This guide is not legal or tax advice.

## What does it take to organise a festival?

**Definition:** A festival is a multi-act event over one or more days, usually outdoors, where the organiser runs programme, site, catering, safety and often camping as one operation. Legally it is often a concert for licensing and VAT, and a temporary venue for building and safety rules.

Before you contact a single act, fix five parameters, because they drive almost every cost line: **size** (permit route, safety plan, entry lanes), **duration** (camping, showers, day tickets), **site** (power, water, access, neighbours), **genre** (fees, volume, age limits, VAT rate) and **legal entity** (liability, tax, grants).

A first festival of 300 to 1,500 visitors on a site you know is realistic for a small team with named leads for programme, production, safety, bar, ticketing and finance, and volunteers.

## Which permits does a festival need in Europe?

Permit law is national, often regional or municipal, but the building blocks are similar across Europe. Your first meeting with the local authority (council, _Ordnungsamt_, _mairie_, _gemeente_) is the most important appointment of the project.

| Area                          | What it covers                                                          | Typical authority                         |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Event notification or licence | The event itself on this site and date                                  | Municipality or council                   |
| Temporary structures          | Stages, tents, grandstands: certification, inspection before use        | Building control                          |
| Safety and crowd plan         | Capacity, escape routes, stewarding, evacuation, communication, weather | Municipality with police and fire service |
| Noise                         | Limits at the nearest homes, curfew, monitoring                         | Environmental health or noise authority   |
| Medical cover                 | First aid and ambulance cover based on a risk assessment                | Municipality or ambulance service         |
| Alcohol                       | Temporary licence if the site has none                                  | Licensing authority                       |

Country examples, as of November 2026:

- **Germany:** the model venue regulation (MVStättV) applies to outdoor venues with stage areas for more than 1,000 visitors. A safety concept is mandatory above 5,000 visitor places, and authorities may require one below that. For "rare events" on up to 18 days a year, noise guidelines allow 70 dB(A) by day and 55 dB(A) at night outside the nearest window.
- **UK (England and Wales):** a Temporary Event Notice only covers fewer than 500 people at all times, staff included, filed at least 10 clear working days ahead. Larger festivals need a premises licence route agreed with the council.
- **Elsewhere:** Ireland, France, the Netherlands, Belgium and others set their own thresholds and lead times. Ask the authority for a written list of required documents.

### Safety plan and medical cover

Write a safety plan even where not mandatory: capacity per area, escape routes, stewards, evacuation, who stops the show, radio channels, weather. Size medical cover by risk assessment. For entry flow, see [event entry management](/en/guides/event-entry-queue-management) for how to calculate lanes and queue times.

## How do you book acts, and what is artist tax?

Book the headliner first, because they sell the early bird tickets, then complementary acts and local openers. Budget three extras on top of every fee:

- **Withholding tax for foreign performers.** Many countries tax non-resident artists where they perform, withheld by the organiser. In Germany it is 15 % of the fee plus 5.5 % solidarity surcharge on that tax, 15.825 % in total (§ 50a EStG). No tax is withheld if income per performance does not exceed €250. Treaties and other countries' rates differ, so check with the local tax office.
- **Social levies** where they exist, e.g. the German artists' social levy (KSA): 4.9 % in 2026, 5.0 % in 2027.
- **Rider costs:** backline, hotel, catering and transfers.

**Worked example: grossing up a net deal.** An agent sells a non-resident headliner for €12,000 "net of tax". To pay €12,000 after withholding in Germany, the contract fee is €12,000 ÷ (1 − 0.15825) = €14,256.01. You withhold €2,138.40 tax and €117.61 surcharge, so the deal costs you €2,256.01 more than the headline number. Always ask if a fee is gross or net.

### Music licensing

Public festivals need a licence from the national collecting society: GEMA (Germany), AKM (Austria), SACEM (France), Buma/Stemra (Netherlands), Unisono (Belgium, the joint licensing service of Sabam, PlayRight and Simim), IMRO (Ireland), PPL PRS (UK), SIAE (Italy) and SGAE (Spain). In Germany, concert tariff U-K is 5.75 % of net box office for up to 2,000 visitors (2026 tariff; 2027 rates not yet published).

## Which ticket types work for a festival?

| Ticket type                  | Purpose                                        | Allocation in the example              |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Early bird weekend           | Cash for deposits, signal to acts and sponsors | 300, separate type, ends when sold out |
| Weekend ticket               | Main product                                   | 900                                    |
| Day ticket Saturday / Sunday | Local and family visitors                      | 300 each                               |
| Camping                      | Overnight stay, priced on its own costs        | 600                                    |

Three rules:

1. **Early bird is a limited ticket type, not a date.** When the 300 are gone, it is sold out. See [early bird tickets](/en/guides/early-bird-tickets) for allocation sizes.
2. **Count capacity per day, not per ticket.** Weekend tickets plus that day's day tickets plus guest list and crew must not exceed the permitted capacity.
3. **Camping is its own product** with its own capacity and costs. State whether it requires a festival ticket.

## How should entry and wristbands work?

Scan each ticket once at first entry and fit a wristband, which proves entitlement for the rest of the festival.

- **Colours per ticket type**, plus one for under-18s so bar staff can refuse spirits.
- **Fit the wristband yourself**; loose bands get passed on.
- **Separate lanes** for camping arrivals with luggage checks.
- **Test connectivity:** mobile networks in open fields overload quickly. Plan dedicated Wi-Fi or a router with an external antenna at entry.

Details for browser-based scanning are in [QR code ticket check-in without an app](/en/guides/qr-code-ticket-check-in).

## What belongs in the weather and sustainability plan?

### Weather

Set thresholds and decision makers in advance, not on the day:

- Who monitors the weather and who decides on interrupting the show
- Wind speeds at which roofs, banners and tents are secured or cleared (from your stage supplier's documents)
- Thunderstorm shelter and routes, heat (free water, shade) and heavy rain (ground, cables, parking)

If you cancel, buyers are usually entitled to a refund: see [event cancellation and refunds in the EU](/en/guides/event-cancellation-refunds-eu).

### Sustainability

- Deposit cups and a camper waste deposit
- Station shuttle, car-sharing, paid parking
- Grid power instead of diesel where possible, tap water stations

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to organise a small festival?

In our worked example for 1,500 visitors a day over two days, costs are about €132,000 net before contingency, led by artist fees, stage production, security and bar stock. A one-day event for 300 people costs a fraction but needs the same basic permits.

### How far in advance should you plan a festival?

Plan 12 to 18 months ahead. Fix site, date and headliner about a year before so early bird sales can start, and file permit applications three to six months ahead at the latest.

### Which permits do I need for a festival?

Usually an event licence or notification, approval of temporary structures, an alcohol licence, a noise permit and, depending on size, a safety plan and medical cover. Thresholds differ: Germany mandates a safety concept above 5,000 visitor places; a UK Temporary Event Notice only works below 500 people.

### Do I have to withhold tax from foreign artists?

In many countries, yes. In Germany you withhold 15.825 % in total when the fee per performance exceeds €250. Other countries have their own rates, so check locally or with a tax adviser.

### Is a camping ticket worth it?

Camping brings revenue and makes weekend tickets more attractive, but costs space, showers, night security and waste handling. Sell it as its own ticket type and price it on those costs; if it cannot cover them, skip camping in year one.
