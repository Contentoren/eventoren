To plan an event, work backwards from the event day through ten phases: concept, budget, venue, permits, ticketing, marketing, staff, tech, the day itself and wrap-up. For a public ticketed event allow at least three to six months; small formats can work in eight weeks, festivals need a year or more. This checklist gives every task a deadline in weeks before the event (T−12 means twelve weeks before), a named owner and a link to the guide that explains the details. Figures and rules are as of November 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Plan backwards from the event day. Book the scarcest things first (venue, headline act), order wristbands and decoration last.
> - Permit deadlines are local. Contact the municipality as soon as date and venue are fixed and ask for its written list of requirements.
> - Start presale 8 to 12 weeks out with a small, limited early bird ticket type, and only after venue and main act are under contract.
> - Every task needs exactly one owner. A simple responsibility matrix prevents most failures on the day.
> - After the event come the obligations: music licence set lists, artist levies and withholding tax where they apply, accounts, and deleting attendee data you no longer need.

## How far ahead should you plan an event?

**Definition:** The **lead time** is the period between deciding to hold an event and the event day. It depends less on guest numbers than on how scarce your venue, acts and permits are.

| Event type                             | Typical lead time | Bottleneck             |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------------- |
| Reading, comedy, small gig (up to 150) | 8–12 weeks        | Artist availability    |
| Party, club night                      | 6–10 weeks        | Venue at weekends      |
| Community or club festival             | 3–4 months        | Permits, volunteers    |
| Concert, 300+ capacity                 | 4–6 months        | Booking, presale       |
| Conference, corporate event            | 4–6 months        | Venue, speakers        |
| Graduation ball, gala                  | 12–18 months      | Venue, catering        |
| Festival                               | 12–18 months      | Site, permits, line-up |

These are rules of thumb, not legal requirements. Peak dates such as New Year's Eve, December weekends or the Eurovision final on 15 May 2027 are booked out earlier.

## Event planning timeline: what happens when?

The "Week" column counts back from the event day (T). T−24 means 24 weeks before, T+2 two weeks after.

| Week         | Milestone                                                                                                                   |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| T−26 to T−20 | Goal, audience, format, date; draft budget; shortlist and visit venues                                                      |
| T−20 to T−16 | Venue contract; book main act or speakers; first contact with the municipality; decide who is legally the organiser         |
| T−16 to T−12 | Ticket prices and types; set up ticket shop and get your organisation approved; request quotes for tech, catering, security |
| T−12 to T−10 | Presale with early bird; marketing plan; file permit applications                                                           |
| T−10 to T−6  | Main marketing push; supplier contracts; liability insurance; recruit volunteers                                            |
| T−6 to T−4   | Register music use with the collecting society; safety and entry plan; draft running order                                  |
| T−4 to T−2   | Staff rota, guest list, door sales; final tech rider; last ticket tier                                                      |
| T−1          | Briefings, materials, scanner test, weather and emergency plan, info email to guests                                        |
| T            | Build, run, get-out                                                                                                         |
| T+1          | Settle door and bar, record damage, thank guests and helpers                                                                |
| T+2          | Check and pay invoices, plan vs actual, analyse feedback                                                                    |
| T+4 to T+6   | Set lists and statements to the collecting society, final accounts                                                          |

## The master checklist by phase

Each item starts with the latest sensible deadline. If a phase does not apply to your event, skip it deliberately and tick it anyway.

### 1. Concept

- [ ] **T−26** Set the goal: profit, cost recovery, fundraising, community or marketing
- [ ] **T−26** Define audience, expected attendance and age limit
- [ ] **T−24** Describe format and flow in one sentence ("Gig with support, 300 standing, 20:00 to 23:00")
- [ ] **T−24** Check the date against school and public holidays and competing events
- [ ] **T−20** Decide who is legally the organiser and signs contracts (association, company, individual)

### 2. Budget

- [ ] **T−24** Collect cost lines: venue, fees, tech, staff, licences, insurance, marketing, ticketing
- [ ] **T−20** Add a contingency of at least 10 % of fixed costs
- [ ] **T−16** Calculate break-even: how many tickets at what price?
- [ ] **T−16** Check VAT on admission in the event's country (e.g. 7 % DE, 13 % AT, 9 % NL, 6 % BE, 5.5 % FR for concerts)
- [ ] **T−12** Plan cash flow: which deposits are due before presale income arrives?

Use the [event budget template](/en/guides/event-budget-template) for cost lines and cash flow, and [how to price event tickets](/en/guides/how-to-price-event-tickets) for break-even.

### 3. Venue

- [ ] **T−24** Requirements: capacity, seating, bar, tech, step-free access, transport links
- [ ] **T−22** Visit two or three venues; request floor plan and licensed capacity
- [ ] **T−20** Contract with build and get-out times, curfew, deposit, cancellation, drinks tie
- [ ] **T−20** Ask whether the venue's music licence covers external organisers

### 4. Permits and compliance

- [ ] **T−20** Contact the municipality: notification, licence, deadlines
- [ ] **T−12** Apply for a temporary alcohol licence if the venue has none
- [ ] **T−12** Apply for use of public space or temporary structures
- [ ] **T−8** Request late-hours or noise exemptions if needed
- [ ] **T−6** Register music use with the collecting society (GEMA, AKM, SACEM, Buma/Stemra, Unisono, IMRO, PPL PRS, SIAE, SGAE)
- [ ] **T−6** Safety plan if the authority or your size requires one

### 5. Ticketing

- [ ] **T−16** Choose a platform, compare fees including payment processing, register your organisation
- [ ] **T−14** Create ticket types with allocations: early bird, regular, concession, door
- [ ] **T−14** Ticket page: total price, start and end, age limit, entry rules, accessibility info
- [ ] **T−12** Open presale
- [ ] **T−4** Create the guest list with quotas
- [ ] **T−2** Plan door sales: prices, payment method, staff

### 6. Marketing

- [ ] **T−14** Event page, images, short description, ticket link
- [ ] **T−12** Announce on all channels; email past attendees only with consent or within the soft opt-in
- [ ] **T−10** Print posters and flyers; artists and partners share the link
- [ ] **T−6** Second wave: programme details, line-up, quotes
- [ ] **T−1** Final info: directions, door time, door price

### 7. Staff and volunteers

- [ ] **T−10** Headcount per area: door, box office, bar, cloakroom, stewards, build and get-out
- [ ] **T−8** Recruit helpers; check pay rules (e.g. German minimum wage €14.60 in 2027)
- [ ] **T−8** Book licensed security where required
- [ ] **T−3** Shift rota with names and phone numbers
- [ ] **T−1** Briefing: procedures, emergency numbers, age checks, who decides what

### 8. Tech

- [ ] **T−12** Collect artists' tech riders
- [ ] **T−10** Book sound, light, stage and power
- [ ] **T−4** Test internet at the entrance (mobile data or Wi-Fi) and arrange a backup hotspot
- [ ] **T−1** Soundcheck time, tech contact, spare cables

### 9. The day

- [ ] **T−2** Minute-by-minute running order with owners
- [ ] **T−1** Emergency plan: escape routes, first aid, weather, power cut
- [ ] **T** Venue walk-through, escape routes clear, notices posted (age policy, house rules)
- [ ] **T** Open doors, monitor capacity continuously
- [ ] **T** Interim cash-up at bar, secure takings

### 10. Wrap-up

- [ ] **T+1** Get-out, returns, record damage
- [ ] **T+1** Thank-you email to attendees with a feedback question
- [ ] **T+2** Check invoices, plan vs actual, establish profit or loss
- [ ] **T+2** Write down lessons learned
- [ ] **T+6** Submit set lists to the collecting society (in Germany: 6 weeks for party tariffs, 21 working days for concerts)
- [ ] **Annual** Report artist levies and withholding tax where they apply (in Germany KSA by 31 March of the following year)
- [ ] **Ongoing** Keep accounting records for the legal period; delete attendee data you no longer need

## Who does what? The responsibility matrix

**Definition:** A **responsibility matrix** (based on RACI) assigns each task to one person who is **accountable** (A), people who **contribute** (C) and people who are **informed** (I). Each row has exactly one A.

| Task                          | Project lead | Finance | Programme | Marketing | Door and volunteers |
| ----------------------------- | ------------ | ------- | --------- | --------- | ------------------- |
| Concept and date              | A            | C       | C         | I         | I                   |
| Budget and break-even         | C            | A       | I         | I         | –                   |
| Venue contract                | A            | C       | I         | –         | I                   |
| Permits, music licence        | A            | I       | C         | –         | I                   |
| Artists and programme         | I            | C       | A         | I         | –                   |
| Ticket types and prices       | C            | A       | I         | C         | I                   |
| Promotion and social media    | I            | I       | C         | A         | –                   |
| Volunteer rota                | I            | –       | –         | –         | A                   |
| Entry, door sales, guest list | I            | C       | –         | –         | A                   |
| Settlement and tax            | I            | A       | I         | –         | C                   |

In a small team one person covers several columns. What matters is that everyone knows who decides each task.

## Which legal questions apply to every event?

This guide is not legal or tax advice. Rules are national; the questions are the same everywhere (as of November 2026):

| Question                                    | Short answer                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Do I have to notify or license the event?   | Depends on municipality and size; alcohol sales without a licensed venue need a temporary licence                           |
| Do I need a music licence?                  | Yes for public events with protected music, from the national collecting society, even without entry fees in many countries |
| Is there a right of withdrawal for tickets? | No 14-day withdrawal for dated leisure events in the EU (CRD Art. 16(l)); refunds are due if you cancel                     |
| Must prices include fees?                   | Yes, show the total price including unavoidable fees from the first price displayed                                         |
| Does my ticket shop need to be accessible?  | Since 28 June 2025 under the European Accessibility Act, unless you are a microenterprise                                   |
| Do I need liability insurance?              | Strongly recommended; venues and authorities often require it                                                               |

## Worked example: a 250-capacity concert in Vienna

**Worked example** with assumed costs: a cultural association plans a concert in Vienna on Saturday 8 May 2027.

| Date                | Week       | Task                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mid-November 2026   | T−25       | Band enquiry, venue visit                                                                                                                          |
| Early December 2026 | T−22       | Band and venue contracts, date fixed                                                                                                               |
| Mid-January 2027    | T−16       | Budget final: fixed costs €4,000 incl. 10 % contingency                                                                                            |
| Mid-January 2027    | T−16       | Break-even: €22 ticket at 13 % Austrian VAT leaves €19.47, minus €1.35 Eventoren fee = €18.12; 221 tickets needed (music licence not yet included) |
| Mid-February 2027   | T−12       | Presale opens with 60 early bird tickets                                                                                                           |
| March–April 2027    | T−8 to T−4 | Marketing, AKM registration, volunteers                                                                                                            |
| Saturday 8 May 2027 | T          | Concert                                                                                                                                            |

## Frequently asked questions

### How many weeks before an event should I start planning?

For small events up to 150 guests, 8 to 12 weeks are often enough; concerts, community festivals and conferences need 3 to 6 months. Festivals and galas need a year or more. The scarcity of venue and acts decides.

### What should an event planning checklist include?

At least concept, budget, venue, permits, ticketing, marketing, staff, tech, the day itself and wrap-up. Every task needs a deadline and one owner. Without those two, a checklist is just a to-do list.

### When should ticket sales start?

For most events 8 to 12 weeks ahead, much earlier for large concerts and festivals. A limited early bird allocation creates early sales and planning certainty. Don't open sales before venue and main act are under contract.

### Do I need a music licence for every event?

For every public event using protected music, yes, from the collecting society in the event's country, often even without an entry fee. An event is generally public when guests are not all personally connected with each other or the organiser. Purely private parties are usually exempt.

### What do I do after the event?

Settle the accounts, check invoices, compare plan and actual, and review feedback. Then handle the obligations: set lists for the music licence, artist levies or withholding tax where applicable, record retention and deleting attendee data you no longer need.
