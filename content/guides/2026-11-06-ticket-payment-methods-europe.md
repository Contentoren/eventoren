Every European ticket shop needs cards (Visa, Mastercard) plus Apple Pay and Google Pay, because most ticket purchases happen spontaneously on a phone. On top of that, each country has a local favourite that can make or break conversion: Bancontact in Belgium, iDEAL (now becoming Wero) in the Netherlands, BLIK in Poland, Bizum in Spain, TWINT in Switzerland, and PayPal and SEPA direct debit in Germany. For organisers, the price of each method matters less than the risk: a direct debit can be reversed for eight weeks, long after the show. This guide maps the methods by country and compares costs and chargeback rules, plus the 2026/27 changes from Wero, the Instant Payments Regulation and PSD3, as of November 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Baseline for 2026: cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay. Then add the local method of each country you sell into.
> - Local leaders by estimated online preference: Bancontact 73 % in Belgium, iDEAL 66 % in the Netherlands, PayPal 67 % in Germany. These are estimates for e-commerce in general.
> - The Netherlands began moving from iDEAL to Wero on 26 October 2026, with completion targeted by the end of 2027. Wero has about 60 M users across Europe.
> - SEPA direct debits can be reversed without reason for 8 weeks, and unauthorised payments for up to 13 months. That's a real risk for tickets.
> - You may not surcharge consumers for cards or SEPA payments anywhere in the EU.

## Which payment methods do European ticket buyers expect?

A **payment method** is the way a buyer pays at checkout: card, digital wallet, bank redirect, direct debit, buy-now-pay-later or account-to-account transfer. Ticket purchases have three traits that shape the choice. They're often made on a phone within minutes of seeing a post, amounts are small to medium, and the service is delivered weeks later. Checkout must therefore be fast, and you need to know how long a payment can still be reversed.

The figures below are **estimates** from Ecommerce News Europe (page updated 10 March 2026). They show the share of online shoppers naming a method as preferred, for e-commerce in general rather than tickets, with multiple answers allowed and no named underlying survey. The cheapest methods on paper, iDEAL | Wero and SEPA direct debit, sit at opposite ends on risk. A Wero or iDEAL payment is an instant credit transfer the buyer pushes, while a direct debit is pulled from the buyer's account and can be reversed weeks later.

### Example: what one lost chargeback costs

Stripe charges €20.00 for each card dispute received (€15.00 for SEPA direct debit disputes). Example calculation: an order of 2 × €25 is disputed after the event and the dispute is lost. You lose €50 of revenue plus €20 in fees, €70 in total. At a margin of €5 per ticket, you need 14 extra tickets to make that back.

## How risky are direct debits, cards and buy-now-pay-later for tickets?

### SEPA direct debit: 8 weeks to reverse

Under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2), a buyer can ask their bank to refund an authorised direct debit **within 8 weeks** of the debit, without giving a reason (Art. 76 and 77). For **unauthorised** debits, such as someone else's IBAN, the deadline is **13 months** (Art. 71). For tickets, that means:

- A buyer can attend the event and then reverse the payment.
- Fraud with stolen account details may only show up after the event.
- Some platforms therefore deliver tickets only after collection, or limit direct debit to longer presales.

Direct debit works well for club members, schools and long presales with a known audience. It's the weakest choice for a club night on sale until the doors open.

### Cards: chargebacks and 3-D Secure

Cardholders can dispute payments through their bank, for example for fraud, a service not provided, or a cancelled event without a refund. Card scheme rules set the reasons and deadlines. What helps:

- **Strong Customer Authentication (3-D Secure)** makes fraud with stolen card details harder. Liability for fraud disputes is set by the card schemes and your provider contract.
- **A recognisable statement descriptor**, so buyers recognise the charge.
- **Reachable support** and quick refunds when you cancel, so buyers don't go to their bank instead.

### Klarna and buy now, pay later

Klarna pays you and handles collecting from the buyer. Who carries which risk is set by the merchant agreement with Klarna or your payment provider. The new Consumer Credit Directive (EU) 2023/2225 applies from **20 November 2026**. It broadly brings buy-now-pay-later credit into scope, and its duties fall mainly on the lender, not the event organiser. That's our reading, not legal advice.

## What's changing with Wero, instant payments and PSD3 in 2026/27?

### Wero: Europe's bank wallet

**Wero** is a payment wallet from the European Payments Initiative (EPI), owned by banks and payment providers and based on SEPA instant credit transfers from the buyer's current account. Status as of November 2026:

| Market      | Wero status                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Germany     | online payments since late 2025; more than 10 M registrations in Germany (end of October 2026, per S-Payment); in-store from 2027. CTS Eventim accepts Wero                      |
| Belgium     | e-commerce since March 2026 (ING, KBC, BNP Paribas Fortis first); in-store live                                                                                                  |
| Luxembourg  | e-commerce and in-store live                                                                                                                                                     |
| France      | progressive e-commerce rollout through 2026; in-store planned for H2 2027                                                                                                        |
| Netherlands | iDEAL → Wero migration started 26 Oct 2026 with 12 banks; target completion 31 Dec 2027; pricing aligned with iDEAL until 31 Dec 2028; Dutch purchase protection from 1 Jan 2028 |
| Austria     | planned                                                                                                                                                                          |

Wero has about 60 M users across Europe. A link with Bizum, Bancomat, MB WAY and Vipps MobilePay under the EuroPA initiative aims at about 130 M users in 13 countries. If you sell to Dutch buyers, the practical step is to make sure your provider supports "iDEAL | Wero" now, because plain iDEAL will be gone by the end of 2027.

### Instant Payments Regulation

Under Regulation (EU) 2024/886, euro-area banks must receive instant SEPA transfers since 9 January 2025 and send them, with free **Verification of Payee** (an IBAN–name check), since 9 October 2025. Instant transfers may not cost more than standard ones. Payment and e-money institutions in the euro area follow on **9 April 2027**, and non-euro Member States on 9 January 2027 (receive) and 9 July 2027 (send). For organisers, bank-transfer presales now settle in seconds. Make sure your account name matches the name you give buyers, or they'll see a mismatch warning.

### PSD3 and the Payment Services Regulation

The EU's new payments package passed the Council's first reading on 29 October 2026 (27–0), and Parliament's second reading is pending. It will apply about 21 months after entry into force, roughly late 2028. Expect an IBAN–name check, more liability for spoofing fraud, e-money institutions merged into payment institutions, and more fee transparency.

## Can you charge extra for certain payment methods?

No, not for the most common ones. EU law forbids surcharges on consumer cards with regulated interchange fees and on SEPA credit transfers and direct debits (PSD2 Art. 62(4)). For any other method, a fee may not exceed your actual cost (Consumer Rights Directive Art. 19). National law can be stricter, as in Germany. A general booking fee that is the same for every method is different. It's allowed, but it must be included in the total price shown to the buyer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which payment methods does a ticket shop need at minimum?

Cards (Visa and Mastercard), Apple Pay and Google Pay cover most mobile ticket purchases in Europe. Add at least one non-card option that your main audience trusts: Bancontact in Belgium, iDEAL | Wero in the Netherlands, PayPal or SEPA direct debit in Germany, BLIK in Poland.

### What is Wero and should I offer it?

Wero is a European bank wallet from EPI, based on instant SEPA transfers, with about 60 M users. It's live online in Germany, Belgium and Luxembourg, rolling out in France and replacing iDEAL in the Netherlands by the end of 2027. If you sell to Dutch buyers, it soon becomes essential. Elsewhere, it's growing but not yet a must.

### Is SEPA direct debit risky for tickets?

Yes, mostly for short presales. Buyers can reverse a direct debit for 8 weeks without a reason and unauthorised debits for up to 13 months, often after the event. For members' events with long lead times, the risk is lower.

### Can I charge a fee for card payments in the EU?

Not to consumers. PSD2 bans surcharges on regulated consumer cards and on SEPA payments, and for other methods any fee may not exceed your cost. A general booking fee that is the same for all methods and shown in the total price stays allowed.

### Does Eventoren support PayPal, iDEAL or Wero?

No. Eventoren supports cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, Klarna, SEPA direct debit and Bancontact. If PayPal, iDEAL or Wero are essential for your audience, choose a platform that supports them.
