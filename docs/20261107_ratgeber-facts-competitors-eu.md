## 3. EU/UK rules 2026–27

### 3.1 Consumer / pricing

- **Digital Fairness Act (DFA):** no legal text yet. Commission proposal scheduled for the College meeting **11 Nov 2026** (consumer package with the CPC revision), as tentative agenda items. Expected topics: dark patterns, addictive design, subscription traps, unfair personalisation and pricing, influencer marketing. Ticketing angles (drip pricing, "from" prices under dynamic pricing, resale) come from the Fitness Check and MEP calls. Law firms expect it in force **end of 2028 at the earliest**. [S32][S33][S34]
- **CRD Art. 16(l)** (Dir. 2011/83/EU): no right of withdrawal for "services related to leisure activities if the contract provides for a specific date or period of performance". This covers dated event tickets, so there is no 14-day withdrawal. Undated vouchers and open tickets are not covered. [S35]
- **Omnibus Dir. (EU) 2019/2161**, applicable since 28 May 2022:
  - CRD info duty when a price is **personalised by automated decision-making**. Recital 45 says plain dynamic or real-time pricing is not caught unless personalised. [S36]
  - **Price Indication Directive 98/6/EC Art. 6a**: any announced price reduction must show the lowest price of the prior **30 days**. This matters for "early bird −20 %" claims. [S36]
  - **Drip pricing** (mandatory fees revealed late) is already unfair under the UCPD (misleading omission, total price incl. unavoidable fees). The DFA is expected to tighten this. German rule: PAngV "Gesamtpreis". [S36][S34]
- **UCPD Annex I No. 23a** (inserted by 2019/2161) is a blacklisted practice: "Reselling events tickets to consumers if the trader acquired them by using automated means to circumvent any limit imposed on the number of tickets that a person can buy or any other rules applicable to the purchase of tickets." [S36]

**Place of supply (VAT Directive 2006/112/EC, consolidated 14 Apr 2025)** [S69]:

- **Art. 53 (B2B):** admission to cultural, artistic, sporting, scientific, educational, entertainment or similar events, plus ancillary services, is taxed where the event **actually takes place**. Since 1 Jan 2025 (Dir. (EU) 2022/542), this "shall not apply … where the attendance is virtual", so virtual B2B admission falls under the general B2B rule (customer's establishment, Art. 44).
- **Art. 54(1) (B2C):** services relating to such activities are taxed where the activities take place, but **from 1 Jan 2025**, where they are "streamed or otherwise made virtually available", the place of supply is **where the consumer is established, has his permanent address or usually resides** (OSS-reportable).
- **Practical:** a German organiser selling tickets for a physical event in Vienna owes Austrian VAT (13 %). Since 1 Jul 2021, B2C admission can be declared via the **Union OSS** (EU-established supplier, event in a Member State where it is not established); the Commission's OSS explanatory notes (§3.1.3 examples, referenced by §3.2.2 for the Union scheme) list "admission to cultural, artistic, sporting, scientific, educational, entertainment or similar events". Alternative: local registration in AT. If OSS is used, all eligible B2C supplies must go through it; no €10,000 threshold applies; input VAT on local costs is reclaimed via the refund procedure or a local return, not OSS; B2B admission is not covered. Non-EU organisers can use the non-Union OSS. (Corrected 2026-11-07: an earlier version wrongly said "no OSS for physical admissions".) [S89] Livestream tickets to consumers are taxed at each buyer's country rate via OSS.
